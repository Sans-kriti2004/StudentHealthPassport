HCL JIGSAW · EDITION 7
Round 2: Show Your App
Grade 6 · Medical Innovation & Smart Healthcare · Student Health Passport — Student Instructions
Part A — Build your app
Your prototype is a Student Health Passport app: a one-page digital card for each student with the 5 most important health details, which the school nurse updates once a year. It must be something we can open with a link.
Privacy and consent — the most important rules
Get WRITTEN consent from a parent or guardian before you enter any real student's health details, even in your pilot.
The link and the screens you share with us must show ONLY made-up demo students (e.g. 'Aarav S. – Demo'). Never share real names, phone numbers or health details.
Keep real pilot data private: only your team and the school nurse should see it. Delete it after the pilot if the school does not want to keep it.
Do not ask for anything you don't need. The 5 key details are enough.
Part B — Add 3 screens and your link
Use screenshots of your app, or clear wireframes (hand-drawn wireframes are fine if you photograph them neatly).
Put your app link on Slide 2. Check that it opens for anyone with the link, from a different phone.
Use real screens of YOUR app only — no pictures from the internet.
Part C — Fill in the PPT template
Rules for the PPT
Do not delete slides. Answer every question.
Write in your own words. Simple English is perfectly fine — ideas matter more than grammar.
Part D — Record your pitch video
Length: 2 to 3 minutes (not more than 3).  Audience: imagine you are presenting to your school principal and school nurse.
Every team member must speak in the video.
Use only demo students on screen — never show a real student's health card in the video.
Record with the phone held sideways (landscape) and kept steady. Choose a quiet place and speak loudly and slowly.
Get permission from your parents or guardians before recording. Use first names only.
Upload both the PPT and the Video to the HCL Jigsaw Portal using your login credentials
One Team Member can upload it and others can approve.
Part E — Submit
Final checklist
All 6 questions answered
3 app screens added, with what each screen does
App link on Slide 2 opens for anyone and shows only demo students
All 5 health details and your privacy and consent rules on Slide 6
Pilot numbers from 10 consented students on Slide 7
Video is 3 minutes or less, every member speaks, and it is uploaded on the portal
How your Submissions will be scored
Each question is scored from 1 to 5, for a total of 30 marks.

Slide | What to do
1 · Title | Do not change anything.
2 · Team & app | Team name, school and city, members with their roles, pitch video link, link to your app and the tool you used. Tick the box only if your link shows made-up demo data.
3 · Q1 Problem | What happens today when a student falls sick at school, which health details are missing, and who will use your app (nurse, teachers, parents).
4 · Q2 Screens | Insert ▸ Pictures and place one screen under each label. Write in the box next to it what that screen does and who uses it.
5 · Q3 How it works | STORE: what each card holds. UPDATE: how the nurse updates it every year and who can edit. USE: how a teacher finds a card quickly in an emergency.
6 · Q4 Details & privacy | One row for each of the 5 health details: why it matters in an emergency, how it is entered (dropdown, tick-box, text) and who can see or edit it. Then write your privacy and consent rules.
7 · Q5 Pilot | Test with 10 real students whose parents have given written consent. Write how many cards you made, how long it took, how fast a card could be found, and what went wrong. Then write what you changed.
8 · Q6 Value & plan | How long it takes today to find a student's health details vs with your app. How the nurse will update all cards every year, and what it costs. Then a 3-line message to your principal.
